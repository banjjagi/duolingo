# DET Booster: 80 → 130 Vocabulary

> **Goal:** Bridge the gap from ~80 (upper B1 / low B2) to ~130 (C1) on the Duolingo English Test.
> At this level, points come less from *more* words and more from *more precise, sophisticated* words —
> upgrading basic vocabulary, using academic terms accurately, and linking ideas with mature connectors.
>
> **How to use:**
> - Sections 1–2 are the highest-leverage: swap basic words for precise ones, and learn connectors that raise your writing/speaking band instantly.
> - Learn each word *in its example sentence* so you can produce it, not just recognize it.
> - Tick ✅ in front of a row once you can use it in your own sentence.

---

## 1. Basic → Advanced Upgrades (highest leverage)

Replacing overused basic words with precise ones is the fastest way to raise a lexical score.

## 2. Discourse Connectors (raise your writing & speaking band)

Mature linking words signal C1-level control. Memorize a few from each function.

| Connector | IPA | Function / Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| furthermore | /ˌfɜːrðərˈmɔːr/ | adds a point (= moreover) | The plan is costly. *Furthermore*, it is risky. |
| nevertheless | /ˌnevərðəˈles/ | contrast (= even so) | It rained; *nevertheless*, we continued. |
| consequently | /ˈkɑːnsɪkwentli/ | result (= as a result) | He overslept; *consequently*, he missed the bus. |
| in contrast | /ɪn ˈkɑːntræst/ | opposite idea | Sales rose in Asia. *In contrast*, Europe fell. |
| on the other hand | /ɑːn ðə ˈʌðər hænd/ | shows the other side | It's cheap; *on the other hand*, it's fragile. |
| for instance | /fɔːr ˈɪnstəns/ | gives an example | Many cities, *for instance* Seoul, are dense. |
| in other words | /ɪn ˈʌðər wɜːrdz/ | restates / clarifies | He was let go — *in other words*, fired. |
| despite / in spite of | /dɪˈspaɪt/ | contrast + noun | *Despite* the rain, the event went on. |
| whereas | /werˈæz/ | direct contrast | He likes tea, *whereas* she prefers coffee. |
| thereby | /ˌðerˈbaɪ/ | by that means | Costs fell, *thereby* boosting profit. |
| in essence | /ɪn ˈesns/ | summarizing the core | *In essence*, the theory is simple. |
| to a certain extent | /ˌsɜːrtn ɪkˈstent/ | partial agreement | I agree, *to a certain extent*. |
| not only ... but also | — | adds emphasis | *Not only* fast *but also* reliable. |
| as a result of | — | cause | Prices rose *as a result of* demand. |
| in conclusion | /ɪn kənˈkluːʒn/ | signals an ending | *In conclusion*, the policy works. |

## 6. Useful Collocations (word partnerships)

Knowing which words go together is a strong C1 marker. Learn these as whole chunks.

| Collocation | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| draw a conclusion | to reach a conclusion | We can *draw a conclusion* from this. |
| play a role | to have an effect | Diet *plays a role* in health. |
| make progress | to advance | She *made significant progress*. |
| pose a threat | to be a danger | Pollution *poses a threat* to wildlife. |
| meet a deadline | to finish on time | We must *meet the deadline*. |
| raise awareness | to inform people | The campaign *raises awareness*. |
| take into account | to consider | *Take into account* the costs. |
| have an impact on | to affect | Sleep *has an impact on* mood. |
| reach a consensus | to agree | They *reached a consensus*. |
| address an issue | to deal with a problem | We must *address the issue* now. |
| bear in mind | to remember / consider | *Bear in mind* the risks. |

| strike a balance | to find a fair middle | *Strike a balance* between work and rest. |
| shed light on | to clarify | The study *sheds light on* the issue. |

## Study Plan (4 weeks)

| Week | Focus | Daily target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sections 1 & 2 (upgrades + connectors) | 10 items + write 5 sentences |
| 2 | Sections 3 & 4 (verbs + adjectives) | 10 items + speak them aloud |
| 3 | Sections 5 & 6 (nouns + collocations) | 10 items + use 3 in a paragraph |
| 4 | Review all + mock writing/speaking | Re-test weak rows; produce, don't just recognize |

**Key principle for 80 → 130:** the test rewards *production*, not recognition. For every word, write one sentence and say it out loud. Recognizing a word earns nothing on the speaking and writing tasks — using it correctly does.
